Credit Sprint's Guide to Marketing Unit 2: The Marketing Concept and Strategy

CLEP® weight 11%

Great marketing starts with a philosophy: find out what customers need and deliver it at a profit. Strategy turns that philosophy into a plan. This unit covers business orientations, strategic planning at three levels, SWOT, the BCG and Ansoff grids, competitive advantage and the parts of a marketing plan.

What you need to know

  • Business orientations: production (make it cheaply and widely), product (make the best quality), selling (push what you made), marketing concept (find a need and fill it), and societal marketing (meet needs while also protecting society's long-run well-being).
  • The marketing concept has three pillars: customer orientation, integrated effort across the whole firm, and a goal of long-run profit through customer satisfaction.
  • Marketing is a process of creating, communicating and delivering value to customers and building relationships. Value is benefits received compared with cost paid.
  • Relationship marketing builds long-term bonds. Customer lifetime value (CLV) is the profit a customer is expected to bring over the whole relationship. Keeping customers usually costs less than winning new ones.
  • Strategic planning works at three levels: corporate (mission and portfolio), strategic business unit (SBU) and functional (marketing, finance, operations).
  • A mission statement defines the firm's purpose and scope. Objectives should be SMART: specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and time-bound.
  • SWOT analysis sorts internal strengths and weaknesses from external opportunities and threats. Strengths and weaknesses are inside the firm; opportunities and threats come from outside it.
  • The BCG growth-share matrix classifies SBUs by market growth rate and relative market share: stars (high growth, high share), cash cows (low growth, high share), question marks (high growth, low share) and dogs (low growth, low share).
  • Ansoff's product-market grid gives four growth strategies: market penetration (existing product, existing market), market development (existing product, new market), product development (new product, existing market) and diversification (new product, new market).
  • Porter's generic strategies: cost leadership, differentiation and focus. A sustainable competitive advantage is hard for rivals to copy.
  • A marketing plan includes an executive summary, situation analysis, objectives, strategies and tactics, budget, implementation, and evaluation and control.

Formulas and rules

Relative market share = your share ÷ the largest competitor's share
Market share = company sales ÷ total industry sales
Value = benefits ÷ price (or benefits received compared with costs paid)

Exam traps

Watch forThe selling orientation focuses on the seller's needs; the marketing concept focuses on the customer's needs.
Watch forSWOT: strengths and weaknesses are internal. A competitor's new product is a threat, not a weakness.
Watch forAnsoff's 'market development' is the same product in a NEW market. 'Product development' is a NEW product in the existing market.
Watch forA cash cow is low growth and high share. Do not mix it up with a star (high growth, high share).

Key terms

  • Marketing concept: A philosophy that the firm should find and fill customer needs, coordinate all effort and earn a long-run profit.
  • Production orientation: Focus on making products efficiently and widely available.
  • Selling orientation: Focus on pushing what the firm has made through heavy promotion.
  • Societal marketing: Meeting customer needs while protecting society's long-run well-being.
  • Relationship marketing: Building long-term, mutually beneficial ties with customers and partners.
  • Customer lifetime value (CLV): The profit expected from a customer over the whole relationship.
  • Mission statement: A statement of the organization's purpose and scope.
  • Strategic business unit (SBU): A division or product line treated as a separate business for planning.
  • SWOT analysis: Sorting internal strengths and weaknesses and external opportunities and threats.
  • BCG matrix: A portfolio grid of market growth rate against relative market share.
  • Star: High growth, high share. Needs investment to keep leading.
  • Cash cow: Low growth, high share. Produces cash to fund other units.
  • Question mark: High growth, low share. Invest or drop.
  • Dog: Low growth, low share. Usually harvested or dropped.
  • Market penetration: Selling more of existing products in existing markets (Ansoff).
  • Market development: Selling existing products in new markets (Ansoff).
  • Product development: Selling new products in existing markets (Ansoff).
  • Diversification: Selling new products in new markets (Ansoff); the riskiest growth strategy.
  • Cost leadership: A generic strategy of having the lowest costs in the industry.
  • Differentiation: A generic strategy of offering something unique that buyers value.
  • Sustainable competitive advantage: An edge that rivals cannot easily copy.
  • Marketing plan: A written document describing the situation, objectives, strategies, tactics, budget and controls.

Practice questions

Five of the 49 Unit 2 questions in Credit Sprint. Every choice is explained in the app.

1. A firm surveys customers first and then designs products around the needs it finds. This firm follows which orientation?
  1. Production orientation
  2. Product orientation
  3. Selling orientation
  4. Marketing concept
Show answer

Marketing concept. The marketing concept starts with customer needs.

2. Customer lifetime value (CLV) is best described as
  1. the cost of one advertisement
  2. the firm's total market share
  3. the profit a customer is expected to bring over the whole relationship
  4. the price of the customer's first purchase
Show answer

the profit a customer is expected to bring over the whole relationship. It counts expected profit across all future purchases.

3. A coffee chain notices a competitor opening next door. In a SWOT analysis this is a
  1. threat
  2. strength
  3. objective
  4. weakness
Show answer

threat. It is an external factor that could hurt the firm.

4. The BCG matrix is based on which two dimensions?
  1. Market growth rate and relative market share
  2. Price and quality
  3. Product and market newness
  4. Strengths and weaknesses
Show answer

Market growth rate and relative market share. These two dimensions define the four cells.

5. A shoe company starts a line of insurance products for a new customer group. This is
  1. product development
  2. market penetration
  3. market development
  4. diversification
Show answer

diversification. A new product in a new market is the riskiest growth strategy.
