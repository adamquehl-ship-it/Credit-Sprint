Credit Sprint's Guide to AP® Calculus AB and BC, Unit 6: Integration and Accumulation of Change

AB weight 15–20%BC weight 15–20%CLEP® about 25%

Unit 6 turns rates into amounts. You approximate accumulated change with Riemann sums and the trapezoidal rule, define the definite integral as a limit of sums, connect integrals and derivatives through the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, and find antiderivatives, including by substitution.

What you need to know

  • The definite integral ∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx is the net signed area between the graph of f and the x-axis from a to b: area above the axis counts positive, area below counts negative.
  • If r(t) is a rate of change, ∫ₐᵇ r(t) dt is the net change in the amount from t = a to t = b. Its units are (units of r) × (units of t).
  • Riemann sums approximate integrals with rectangles: left, right or midpoint. The trapezoidal rule averages left and right sums. Intervals in a table can have unequal widths, so multiply each height by its own width.
  • For an increasing function, a left sum underestimates and a right sum overestimates. For a concave-up function, the trapezoidal rule overestimates and a midpoint sum underestimates.
  • A definite integral is the limit of Riemann sums: ∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx = lim(n→∞) Σ f(xᵢ)·Δx with Δx = (b − a)/n. Be able to translate a sum like Σ (1 + 3i/n)²·(3/n) into an integral.
  • Fundamental Theorem, part 1: if F(x) = ∫ₐˣ f(t) dt, then F′(x) = f(x). With a variable upper limit g(x), d/dx ∫ₐ^g(x) f(t) dt = f(g(x))·g′(x).
  • Fundamental Theorem, part 2: ∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx = F(b) − F(a) for any antiderivative F of f.
  • Accumulation functions: g(x) = ∫ₐˣ f(t) dt increases where f > 0, decreases where f < 0, and is concave up where f is increasing. Values of g are areas under f.
  • Integral properties: ∫ₐᵃ f = 0, ∫ₐᵇ f = −∫ᵦᵃ f, ∫ₐᵇ f + ∫ᵦᶜ f = ∫ₐᶜ f, and constants factor out.
  • Basic antiderivatives: ∫xⁿ dx = xⁿ⁺¹/(n + 1) + C (n ≠ −1), ∫1/x dx = ln|x| + C, ∫eˣ dx = eˣ + C, ∫cos x dx = sin x + C, ∫sin x dx = −cos x + C, ∫sec²x dx = tan x + C, ∫1/(1 + x²) dx = arctan x + C.
  • u-substitution reverses the chain rule: choose u as an inside function whose derivative appears (up to a constant) in the integrand. For a definite integral, change the limits to u-values or switch back to x.
  • Other techniques for AB: long division when the top degree is at least the bottom's, and completing the square to reach an arctan form.
  • (BC) Integration by parts: ∫u dv = uv − ∫v du. Choose u to be the factor that gets simpler when differentiated (LIATE: logs, inverse trig, algebraic, trig, exponential). A tabular layout speeds up repeated parts, such as ∫x³eˣ dx.
  • (BC) Partial fractions: split a rational function with distinct linear factors in the denominator, such as 1/[(x − 1)(x + 2)] = A/(x − 1) + B/(x + 2), then integrate each piece as a logarithm.
  • (BC) Improper integrals have an infinite limit or an infinite discontinuity. Rewrite with a limit: ∫₁^∞ f(x) dx = lim(b→∞) ∫₁ᵇ f(x) dx. It converges if the limit is a finite number.
  • (BC) The p-integral ∫₁^∞ 1/xᵖ dx converges when p > 1 and diverges when p ≤ 1.

Formulas

  • ∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx = F(b) − F(a)
  • d/dx ∫ₐ^g(x) f(t) dt = f(g(x))·g′(x)
  • Trapezoid: ½·Δx·[f(x₀) + f(x₁)] for each subinterval
  • ∫xⁿ dx = xⁿ⁺¹/(n+1) + C; ∫1/x dx = ln|x| + C
  • Net change: A(b) = A(a) + ∫ₐᵇ A′(t) dt
  • ∫u dv = uv − ∫v du
  • ∫₁^∞ 1/xᵖ dx converges ⇔ p > 1

Exam traps

Watch forNet change and total distance are different. ∫ v(t) dt is displacement; ∫ |v(t)| dt is total distance.
Watch forDo not forget + C on indefinite integrals; on the exam a missing + C can cost a point.
Watch forWith u-substitution in a definite integral, change the limits too, or substitute back before evaluating. Mixing x-limits with u is wrong.
Watch forAn accumulation function's value at the starting point is 0 (plus any initial amount you add).
Watch forIn a Riemann sum from a table, use each subinterval's actual width. Unequal widths are common on the exam.
Watch for(BC) An integral like ∫₀² 1/(x − 1) dx is improper because of the asymptote at x = 1 inside the interval. Split it and check each piece; you cannot just evaluate the antiderivative at the endpoints.

Key terms

  • Definite integral: ∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx: the net signed area between f and the x-axis from a to b.
  • Integral of a rate: The net change in the quantity over the interval.
  • Left Riemann sum: Rectangles with heights at left endpoints; underestimates an increasing function.
  • Right Riemann sum: Rectangles with heights at right endpoints; overestimates an increasing function.
  • Midpoint sum: Rectangles with heights at midpoints; underestimates a concave-up function.
  • Trapezoidal rule: Average of the left and right sums; overestimates a concave-up function.
  • Riemann sum limit: ∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx = lim(n→∞) Σ f(xᵢ)Δx, with Δx = (b − a)/n.
  • FTC part 1: d/dx ∫ₐˣ f(t) dt = f(x).
  • FTC part 1 with the chain rule: d/dx ∫ₐ^g(x) f(t) dt = f(g(x))·g′(x).
  • FTC part 2: ∫ₐᵇ f(x) dx = F(b) − F(a), where F′ = f.
  • Accumulation function: g(x) = ∫ₐˣ f(t) dt; g′ = f, g(a) = 0.
  • Reversing limits: ∫ᵦᵃ f = −∫ₐᵇ f.
  • Adding intervals: ∫ₐᵇ f + ∫ᵦᶜ f = ∫ₐᶜ f.
  • Antiderivative of xⁿ: xⁿ⁺¹/(n + 1) + C, for n ≠ −1.
  • Antiderivative of 1/x: ln|x| + C.
  • Antiderivatives of sin x and cos x: ∫sin x dx = −cos x + C; ∫cos x dx = sin x + C.

Practice questions

Five of the 97 Unit 6 questions in Credit Sprint. Every choice is explained in the app.

1. The graph of f lies below the x-axis on [2, 5]. What can you say about ∫ from 2 to 5 of f(x) dx?
  1. It is positive, because area is positive
  2. It is 0
  3. It is undefined
  4. It is negative
Show answer

It is negative. Area below the axis counts negatively in a definite integral.

2. Which expression equals lim(n→∞) Σ from i = 1 to n of (1 + 2i/n)²·(2/n)?
  1. ∫ from 1 to 3 of (1 + x)² dx
  2. ∫ from 1 to 3 of x² dx
  3. ∫ from 0 to 2 of x² dx
  4. ∫ from 0 to 1 of (1 + 2x)² dx
Show answer

∫ from 1 to 3 of x² dx. Δx = 2/n and xᵢ = 1 + 2i/n, so the sum is a right Riemann sum for x² from 1 to 1 + 2 = 3.

3. What is ∫ from 2 to 3 of (4x − 1) dx?
  1. −9
  2. 4
  3. 15
  4. 9
Show answer

9. An antiderivative is F(x) = 2x² − x. By the Fundamental Theorem, F(3) − F(2) = 15 − (6) = 9.

4. What is ∫ from 1 to 3 of (4x² + 3x + 3) dx?
  1. 158/3
  2. 117/2
  3. −158/3
  4. 38
Show answer

158/3. An antiderivative is F(x) = (4/3)x³ + (3/2)x² + 3x. By the Fundamental Theorem, F(3) − F(1) = 117/2 − (35/6) = 158/3.

5. A table gives f(0) = 9; f(2) = 7; f(4) = 13; f(6) = 19; f(7) = 10. Using a right Riemann sum with the four subintervals in the table, approximate ∫ from 0 to 7 of f(x) dx.
  1. 77
  2. 88
  3. 84
  4. 165/2
Show answer

88. Widths: 2, 2, 2, 1. Right sum = 2(7) + 2(13) + 2(19) + 1(10) = 88.
