Credit Sprint's Guide to AP® Calculus BC, Unit 9: Parametric Equations, Polar Coordinates, and Vector-Valued Functions

BC weight 10–15%

Unit 9 moves calculus off the graph of y = f(x). Curves defined parametrically or by vectors carry a particle through the plane over time, and polar curves are drawn by distance and angle. You find slopes, second derivatives, speed, distance traveled, arc length and polar areas.

What you need to know

  • Parametric curves: x = x(t), y = y(t). The slope is dy/dx = (dy/dt) ÷ (dx/dt), wherever dx/dt ≠ 0. Horizontal tangents occur where dy/dt = 0 (and dx/dt ≠ 0); vertical tangents where dx/dt = 0 (and dy/dt ≠ 0).
  • The second derivative of a parametric curve is d²y/dx² = [d/dt (dy/dx)] ÷ (dx/dt). Do not just differentiate dy/dx with respect to t.
  • Arc length of a parametric curve from t = a to t = b: ∫ₐᵇ √[(dx/dt)² + (dy/dt)²] dt. For motion, this is the distance traveled.
  • A vector-valued function r(t) = ⟨x(t), y(t)⟩ gives position. Velocity is v(t) = ⟨x′(t), y′(t)⟩ and acceleration is a(t) = ⟨x″(t), y″(t)⟩.
  • Speed is the magnitude of velocity: |v(t)| = √[(x′(t))² + (y′(t))²]. Total distance traveled is the integral of speed.
  • Position from velocity, one component at a time: x(b) = x(a) + ∫ₐᵇ x′(t) dt and y(b) = y(a) + ∫ₐᵇ y′(t) dt.
  • Polar coordinates: a point is (r, θ), with x = r cos θ and y = r sin θ. A polar graph r = f(θ) is traced as θ sweeps around.
  • Polar slope: write x = f(θ)cos θ and y = f(θ)sin θ, then dy/dx = (dy/dθ) ÷ (dx/dθ).
  • dr/dθ tells whether a point moves away from the origin (dr/dθ > 0 with r > 0) or toward it.
  • Polar area: A = ½∫ᵅᵝ r² dθ. The area between two polar curves is ½∫ (R² − r²) dθ, with limits from the intersection angles.

Formulas

  • dy/dx = (dy/dt)/(dx/dt)
  • d²y/dx² = [d/dt(dy/dx)] / (dx/dt)
  • Speed = √[(x′)² + (y′)²]
  • Arc length = ∫√[(x′)² + (y′)²] dt
  • Polar area = ½∫ r² dθ
  • x = r cos θ, y = r sin θ

Exam traps

Watch ford²y/dx² for a parametric curve needs the extra division by dx/dt. This is the most missed step in the unit.
Watch forSpeed is a scalar. Do not report a vector when the question asks for speed.
Watch forIn the polar area formula, the ½ and the square are both required: ½∫r² dθ, not ∫r dθ.
Watch forFor the area inside one loop or between curves, find the θ-limits by setting r = 0 or setting the two curves equal. Draw a quick sketch to check which curve is outside.

Key terms

  • Parametric curve: x = x(t), y = y(t): a point traced out over time t.
  • Parametric slope: dy/dx = (dy/dt) ÷ (dx/dt).
  • Parametric second derivative: d²y/dx² = [d/dt (dy/dx)] ÷ (dx/dt).
  • Parametric arc length: ∫ₐᵇ √[(dx/dt)² + (dy/dt)²] dt.
  • Position vector: r(t) = ⟨x(t), y(t)⟩.
  • Velocity vector: v(t) = ⟨x′(t), y′(t)⟩.
  • Acceleration vector: a(t) = ⟨x″(t), y″(t)⟩.
  • Speed (vectors): |v(t)| = √[(x′(t))² + (y′(t))²].
  • Distance traveled (vectors): ∫ₐᵇ √[(x′(t))² + (y′(t))²] dt.
  • Polar to rectangular: x = r cos θ, y = r sin θ, r² = x² + y².
  • Polar slope: dy/dx = (dy/dθ) ÷ (dx/dθ), with x = r cos θ and y = r sin θ.
  • Polar area: A = ½∫ᵅᵝ r² dθ.
  • Area between polar curves: ½∫ᵅᵝ (R² − r²) dθ.
  • dr/dθ: Positive with r > 0: the point moves away from the pole. Negative: toward it.

Practice questions

Five of the 48 Unit 9 questions in Credit Sprint. Every choice is explained in the app.

1. x(t) = t², y(t) = t³. What is dy/dx at t = 2?
  1. 3
  2. 3/2
  3. 4/3
  4. 12
Show answer

3. dy/dt = 3t² = 12 and dx/dt = 2t = 4, so dy/dx = 12 ÷ 4 = 3.

2. A particle's velocity is ⟨2t, 1⟩ and its position at t = 0 is ⟨1, 3⟩. What is its position at t = 2?
  1. ⟨5, 5⟩
  2. ⟨4, 2⟩
  3. ⟨5, 3⟩
  4. ⟨4, 1⟩
Show answer

⟨5, 5⟩. x(2) = 1 + ∫₀² 2t dt = 1 + 4 = 5; y(2) = 3 + ∫₀² 1 dt = 5.

3. A curve is given by x(t) = t² + t and y(t) = t⁴. What is dy/dx at t = 3?
  1. 7/108
  2. 108
  3. 108/7
  4. 756
Show answer

108/7. dx/dt = 7 and dy/dt = 108 at t = 3, so dy/dx = 108 ÷ 7 = 108/7.

4. A curve is given by x(t) = t³ + 4t and y(t) = t³. What is dy/dx at t = 3?
  1. 837
  2. 27/31
  3. 31/27
  4. 27
Show answer

27/31. dx/dt = 31 and dy/dt = 27 at t = 3, so dy/dx = 27 ÷ 31 = 27/31.

5. At t = 2, a particle in the plane has x′(2) = 2 and y′(2) = 3. What is its speed at t = 2?
  1. 5
  2. √13
  3. ⟨2, 3⟩
  4. 13
Show answer

√13. Speed = √[(x′)² + (y′)²] = √(4 + 9) = √13.
