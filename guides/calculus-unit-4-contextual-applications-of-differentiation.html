Credit Sprint's Guide to AP® Calculus AB and BC, Unit 4: Contextual Applications of Differentiation

AB weight 10–15%BC weight 5–10%CLEP® about 12%

Unit 4 puts the derivative to work: interpreting a derivative in context with units, motion along a line, related rates, linear approximation and L'Hospital's Rule for indeterminate limits.

What you need to know

  • Interpret f′(a) in a sentence with units: 'At t = 3 hours, the volume is increasing at 40 liters per hour.' The units are output units per input unit.
  • Straight-line motion: position s(t), velocity v(t) = s′(t), acceleration a(t) = v′(t) = s″(t). Speed is |v(t)|.
  • The particle moves right (or up) when v > 0 and left when v < 0. It changes direction when v changes sign.
  • Speed increases when velocity and acceleration have the same sign and decreases when they have opposite signs.
  • Related rates: draw a picture, name the variables, write an equation that relates them (Pythagorean theorem, similar triangles, area or volume formulas), differentiate with respect to t, then substitute the known values. Substitute only after differentiating.
  • Common related-rates set-ups: a ladder sliding down a wall (x² + y² = L²), a spreading circle (A = πr²), a filling cone (V = ⅓πr²h with r tied to h by similar triangles), and a shadow (similar triangles).
  • Linear approximation: f(x) ≈ f(a) + f′(a)(x − a) for x near a. If f is concave up near a, the tangent line lies below the curve, so the approximation is an underestimate; concave down gives an overestimate.
  • L'Hospital's Rule: if lim f(x)/g(x) gives 0/0 or ∞/∞, then it equals lim f′(x)/g′(x), when that limit exists. Differentiate the top and bottom separately; this is not the quotient rule.
  • State the indeterminate form before you use L'Hospital's Rule. On free-response questions you must show that both limits are 0 (or both infinite).

Formulas

  • v(t) = s′(t), a(t) = v′(t), speed = |v(t)|
  • Linearization: L(x) = f(a) + f′(a)(x − a)
  • L'Hospital: 0/0 or ∞/∞ ⇒ lim f/g = lim f′/g′
  • Cone: V = ⅓πr²h; sphere: V = (4/3)πr³, SA = 4πr²

Exam traps

Watch forVelocity and speed are different. A velocity of −5 m/s is a speed of 5 m/s.
Watch forIn related rates, do not plug in a changing quantity's value before differentiating. Constants can go in early; variables cannot.
Watch forA negative rate means decreasing. If the water level falls, dh/dt is negative.
Watch forL'Hospital's Rule only applies to 0/0 or ∞/∞ forms. Check first; using it on 1/0 gives a wrong answer.
Watch forAnswer with units every time a question gives context. A bare number usually loses the point.
Watch for(BC) L'Hospital's Rule also handles 0·∞, ∞ − ∞, 1^∞, 0⁰ and ∞⁰ after you rewrite them as a quotient, often by taking a logarithm first.

Key terms

  • Derivative in context: A rate of change: output units per input unit, at a specific moment.
  • Velocity: v(t) = s′(t): the rate of change of position.
  • Acceleration: a(t) = v′(t) = s″(t): the rate of change of velocity.
  • Speed: |v(t)|: the size of the velocity, with no direction.
  • Speeding up: Velocity and acceleration have the same sign.
  • Slowing down: Velocity and acceleration have opposite signs.
  • Change of direction: The velocity changes sign.
  • Related rates: Relate the quantities with an equation, differentiate with respect to t, then substitute the moment's values.
  • Linear approximation: f(x) ≈ f(a) + f′(a)(x − a) for x near a.
  • Tangent line over- or underestimate: Concave up: the tangent line underestimates. Concave down: it overestimates.
  • L'Hospital's Rule: If lim f/g is 0/0 or ∞/∞, it equals lim f′/g′ (differentiate top and bottom separately).
  • Volume of a cone: V = ⅓πr²h.
  • Volume and surface area of a sphere: V = (4/3)πr³; SA = 4πr².
  • Other indeterminate forms (BC): 0·∞, ∞ − ∞, 1^∞, 0⁰ and ∞⁰: rewrite as a quotient (often with ln) before using L'Hospital's Rule.

Practice questions

Five of the 68 Unit 4 questions in Credit Sprint. Every choice is explained in the app.

1. V(t) is the volume of water in a tank, in liters, t minutes after noon. What does V′(10) = −3 mean?
  1. At 12:10, the tank holds 3 liters less than at noon
  2. At 12:10, the tank contains −3 liters
  3. The volume decreases by 3 liters every 10 minutes
  4. At 12:10, the volume of water is decreasing at 3 liters per minute
Show answer

At 12:10, the volume of water is decreasing at 3 liters per minute. V′(10) is the rate of change of the volume at t = 10, in liters per minute. The negative sign means decreasing.

2. s(t) is position in meters and t is in seconds. What are the units of s″(t)?
  1. seconds per meter
  2. meters per second per second
  3. meters per second
  4. meters
Show answer

meters per second per second. s′ is in meters per second; its derivative s″ is in meters per second per second.

3. A particle moves along the x-axis with position x(t) = t³ − (9/2)t² + 6t for t ≥ 0. At what times is the particle at rest?
  1. t = 1 and t = 2
  2. t = 3 and t = 2
  3. t = 3/2 only
  4. t = 0 only
Show answer

t = 1 and t = 2. v(t) = x′(t) = 3t² − 9t + 6 = 3(t − 1)(t − 2). The particle is at rest when v = 0: t = 1 and t = 2.

4. The radius of a circle grows at 1 cm/s. How fast is the area growing when the radius is 4 cm?
  1. 8π cm²/s
  2. 16π cm²/s
  3. 2π cm²/s
  4. 4π cm²/s
Show answer

8π cm²/s. A = πr², so dA/dt = 2πr·dr/dt = 2π(4)(1) = 8π cm²/s.

5. The radius of a circle grows at 3/2 cm/s. How fast is the area growing when the radius is 12 cm?
  1. 18π cm²/s
  2. 36π cm²/s
  3. 3π cm²/s
  4. 144π cm²/s
Show answer

36π cm²/s. A = πr², so dA/dt = 2πr·dr/dt = 2π(12)(3/2) = 36π cm²/s.
