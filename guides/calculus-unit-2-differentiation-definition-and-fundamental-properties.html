Credit Sprint's Guide to AP® Calculus AB and BC, Unit 2: Differentiation: Definition and Fundamental Properties

AB weight 10–15%BC weight 5–10%CLEP® about 12%

The derivative is the instantaneous rate of change: the slope of the tangent line, found as the limit of slopes of secant lines. You learn the limit definition, when a function is differentiable, and the rules that make derivatives fast: power, constant multiple, sum, product and quotient rules, plus the derivatives of sine, cosine, eˣ and ln x.

What you need to know

  • The average rate of change of f on [a, b] is [f(b) − f(a)] ÷ (b − a), the slope of the secant line. The instantaneous rate of change at x = a is the limit of that slope as b → a.
  • The derivative at a point: f′(a) = lim(h→0) [f(a + h) − f(a)] ÷ h, or equivalently lim(x→a) [f(x) − f(a)] ÷ (x − a). Recognize these forms: a limit that looks like one is the derivative of some function at some point.
  • Notation: f′(x), y′, dy/dx and d/dx[f(x)] all mean the derivative. f′(a) is a number (a slope); f′(x) is a function.
  • The tangent line at x = a is y = f(a) + f′(a)(x − a). Near a, it approximates the curve.
  • Estimate a derivative from a table with a difference quotient using the closest values on either side of the point. Give units: (units of f) per (unit of x).
  • Differentiable implies continuous, but continuous does not imply differentiable. Corners (|x| at 0), cusps, vertical tangents and discontinuities all break differentiability.
  • Power rule: d/dx[xⁿ] = n·xⁿ⁻¹ for any real n. Rewrite roots and reciprocals as powers first: √x = x^(1/2), 1/x³ = x⁻³.
  • Constant, constant multiple and sum rules: the derivative of a constant is 0, d/dx[k·f] = k·f′ and d/dx[f ± g] = f′ ± g′.
  • Know these by heart: d/dx[sin x] = cos x, d/dx[cos x] = −sin x, d/dx[eˣ] = eˣ, d/dx[ln x] = 1/x.
  • Product rule: (fg)′ = f′g + fg′. Quotient rule: (f/g)′ = (f′g − fg′) ÷ g². 'Low d-high minus high d-low, over low squared.'
  • Use the quotient rule (or rewrite) to get the other trig derivatives: d/dx[tan x] = sec²x, d/dx[cot x] = −csc²x, d/dx[sec x] = sec x tan x, d/dx[csc x] = −csc x cot x.

Formulas

  • f′(a) = lim(h→0) [f(a+h) − f(a)] / h
  • Tangent line: y = f(a) + f′(a)(x − a)
  • d/dx[xⁿ] = n·xⁿ⁻¹
  • (fg)′ = f′g + fg′; (f/g)′ = (f′g − fg′)/g²
  • d/dx sin x = cos x; d/dx cos x = −sin x; d/dx tan x = sec²x
  • d/dx eˣ = eˣ; d/dx ln x = 1/x

Exam traps

Watch forThe derivative of a product is not the product of the derivatives. (x²·sin x)′ is 2x·sin x + x²·cos x, not 2x·cos x.
Watch forIn the quotient rule the order matters: f′g − fg′ on top. Flipping it changes the sign.
Watch forA function can be continuous and still not differentiable. Look for corners and cusps in a graph.
Watch forWhen a limit 'looks like' a derivative, identify f and a. lim(h→0) [(3+h)² − 9]/h is f′(3) for f(x) = x², which is 6.
Watch ford/dx[eˣ] = eˣ, but d/dx[xᵉ] = e·xᵉ⁻¹ by the power rule. Read the base and exponent carefully.

Key terms

  • Average rate of change: [f(b) − f(a)] ÷ (b − a): the slope of the secant line.
  • Derivative at a point: f′(a) = lim(h→0) [f(a + h) − f(a)] ÷ h: the slope of the tangent line at x = a.
  • Alternate definition of f′(a): lim(x→a) [f(x) − f(a)] ÷ (x − a).
  • Tangent line: y = f(a) + f′(a)(x − a).
  • Normal line: The line perpendicular to the tangent: slope −1/f′(a).
  • Differentiability: f′(c) exists. It requires continuity; corners, cusps, vertical tangents and breaks prevent it.
  • Differentiable implies continuous: If f′(c) exists then f is continuous at c. The converse is false (|x| at 0).
  • Power rule: d/dx[xⁿ] = n·xⁿ⁻¹.
  • Constant multiple and sum rules: (k·f)′ = k·f′ and (f ± g)′ = f′ ± g′.
  • Product rule: (fg)′ = f′g + fg′.
  • Quotient rule: (f/g)′ = (f′g − fg′) ÷ g².
  • d/dx sin x: cos x.
  • d/dx cos x: −sin x.
  • d/dx tan x: sec² x.
  • d/dx sec x: sec x tan x.
  • d/dx csc x and cot x: −csc x cot x and −csc² x.

Practice questions

Five of the 69 Unit 2 questions in Credit Sprint. Every choice is explained in the app.

1. Which limit is the definition of f′(4) for f(x) = √x?
  1. lim(h→0) [√(4 + h) − 2] ÷ h
  2. lim(x→4) [√x − 4] ÷ (x − 4)
  3. lim(h→0) √(4 + h) ÷ h
  4. lim(h→0) [√(4 + h) − √4] ÷ 4
Show answer

lim(h→0) [√(4 + h) − 2] ÷ h. f′(4) = lim(h→0) [f(4 + h) − f(4)] ÷ h, and f(4) = √4 = 2.

2. Which statement is true for every function f?
  1. If f is differentiable at c, then f is continuous at c
  2. If f is continuous at c, then f is differentiable at c
  3. If f′(c) = 0, then f is not continuous at c
  4. If f(c) is defined, then f′(c) exists
Show answer

If f is differentiable at c, then f is continuous at c. Differentiability implies continuity. The converse is false.

3. If f(x) = −2x⁴ − 6x − 9, what is f′(−1)?
  1. 2
  2. −5
  3. −4
  4. −7
Show answer

2. By the power rule, f′(x) = −8x³ − 6. Then f′(−1) = 2.

4. If f(x) = −3x⁴ + 6x + 7, what is f′(3)?
  1. −318
  2. −218
  3. −311
  4. −75
Show answer

−318. By the power rule, f′(x) = −12x³ + 6. Then f′(3) = −318.

5. Which is an equation of the line tangent to y = x² − 4x at x = −1?
  1. y = −6x − 1
  2. y = −6x + 5
  3. y = 5x − 1
  4. y = (1/6)x + 31/6
Show answer

y = −6x − 1. The point is (−1, 5) and the slope is y′(−1) = −6. So y − 5 = −6(x − −1), which simplifies to y = −6x − 1.
