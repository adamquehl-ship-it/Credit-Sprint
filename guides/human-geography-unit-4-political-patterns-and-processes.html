Credit Sprint's Guide to AP® Human Geography, Unit 4: Political Patterns and Processes

AP® weight 12–17%

How space is divided and governed. Unit 4 covers states, nations and nation-states, the historic forces behind today's political map, types of boundaries and how they are disputed, forms of government, and the forces that unite and divide states, from devolution to supranational organizations.

What you need to know

  • A state is a political unit with a permanent population, defined territory and sovereignty. A nation is a group of people with a shared culture and sense of identity. A nation-state is a state whose people are mostly one nation (Japan, Iceland). A multinational state holds several nations (Russia, the United Kingdom). A multistate nation spreads across several states (Koreans). A stateless nation has no state of its own (Kurds, Palestinians).
  • Territoriality is the connection of people to the land they control. Self-determination is a nation's right to govern itself. Colonialism, imperialism, independence movements and the breakup of empires drew much of today's map, and the end of the Cold War created new states in Eastern Europe and Central Asia.
  • Boundaries by origin: antecedent boundaries were drawn before many people settled; subsequent boundaries were drawn after settlement, often along cultural lines; superimposed boundaries were forced on an area by outside powers (much of Africa after the Berlin Conference); relic boundaries no longer function but leave traces (the Berlin Wall). Geometric boundaries follow straight lines; physical boundaries follow rivers, mountains or lakes.
  • Boundary disputes can be definitional (the wording of a treaty), locational (where the line is), operational (how the border works, such as migration) and allocational (over resources, such as oil or water).
  • Maritime boundaries come from the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea (UNCLOS): territorial seas to 12 nautical miles and exclusive economic zones (EEZs) to 200 nautical miles, where a state controls fishing and mining.
  • Inside states, voting districts are redrawn after each census (redistricting). Gerrymandering draws them to favor one party, by packing opponents into a few districts or cracking them across many.
  • Forms of government: a unitary state concentrates power in the national government (France, Japan). A federal state shares power with regions (the United States, India, Germany). Federal systems can help large or diverse states manage differences.
  • State shapes affect unity and defense: compact (Poland), elongated (Chile), fragmented (Indonesia, the Philippines), perforated (South Africa around Lesotho) and prorupted (Thailand, Namibia). Enclaves are surrounded by another state; exclaves are cut off from their main territory.
  • Devolution moves power from the central government to regions, driven by ethnic, linguistic, religious, economic or physical divisions (Catalonia, Scotland, Quebec). It can lead to balkanization, the breakup of a region into smaller hostile states. Terrorism and ethnic separatism are centrifugal forces.
  • Supranationalism brings states together for shared goals: the United Nations, the European Union, NATO, ASEAN, the African Union and trade blocs such as USMCA. Members give up some sovereignty in exchange for economic, military or political benefits.

Exam traps

Watch forA state and a nation are not the same. The state is the government and its territory; the nation is the people and their shared culture.
Watch forSuperimposed boundaries are drawn by OUTSIDE powers. Subsequent boundaries are drawn after settlement and often follow cultural divides. Many African boundaries are superimposed.
Watch forDevolution weakens central power and is a centrifugal force. Supranationalism joins states together.
Watch forAn exclave is part of a state cut off from the rest (Alaska, Kaliningrad). An enclave is completely surrounded by another state (Lesotho).

Key terms

  • State: A political unit with a permanent population, defined territory, government and sovereignty.
  • Nation: A group of people with a shared culture, history and identity.
  • Nation-state: A state whose population is mostly one nation.
  • Multinational state: A state with two or more nations.
  • Multistate nation: A nation whose people live across several states.
  • Stateless nation: A nation without a state of its own, such as the Kurds.
  • Sovereignty: The right of a government to control its territory without outside interference.
  • Self-determination: The right of a nation to govern itself.
  • Territoriality: The connection of people to, and their control over, a territory.
  • Colonialism: Control of one territory by another state, with settlers and administrators.
  • Imperialism: Control of other territories for economic and political gain.
  • Antecedent boundary: A boundary drawn before an area was heavily settled.
  • Subsequent boundary: A boundary drawn after settlement, often along cultural lines.
  • Superimposed boundary: A boundary forced on an area by outside powers.
  • Relic boundary: A former boundary that no longer functions but leaves traces in the landscape.
  • Geometric boundary: A boundary that follows straight lines, such as lines of latitude.

Practice questions

Five of the 64 Unit 4 questions in Credit Sprint. Every choice is explained in the app.

1. Which combination defines a state?
  1. A shared language and religion
  2. A capital city on the coast
  3. Membership in the United Nations
  4. A permanent population, defined territory, government and sovereignty
Show answer

A permanent population, defined territory, government and sovereignty. These are the standard criteria for statehood.

2. Koreans live in both North Korea and South Korea. Koreans are an example of a
  1. Multinational state
  2. Stateless nation
  3. Enclave
  4. Multistate nation
Show answer

Multistate nation. One nation lives in more than one state.

3. Many African boundaries are straight lines that cut across ethnic homelands. These are
  1. Superimposed boundaries
  2. Antecedent boundaries
  3. Relic boundaries
  4. Subsequent boundaries
Show answer

Superimposed boundaries. Outside powers drew them.

4. The Rio Grande separating Texas and Mexico is an example of a
  1. Physical boundary
  2. Perceptual region
  3. Relic boundary
  4. Geometric boundary
Show answer

Physical boundary. It follows a natural feature.

5. Under the UN Convention on the Law of the Sea, a country's exclusive economic zone extends
  1. 3 nautical miles from its coast
  2. To the middle of every ocean
  3. 200 nautical miles from its coast
  4. 12 nautical miles from its coast
Show answer

200 nautical miles from its coast. It controls fishing, oil and minerals there.
