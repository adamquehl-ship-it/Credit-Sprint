Credit Sprint's Guide to AP® Calculus AB and BC, Unit 3: Differentiation: Composite, Implicit, and Inverse Functions

AB weight 5–10%BC weight 5–10%CLEP® about 10%

Unit 3 handles functions inside other functions. The chain rule differentiates compositions; implicit differentiation finds dy/dx when y is not solved for; and inverse functions, including the inverse trig functions, have derivatives that come straight from the chain rule. You also take second and higher derivatives.

What you need to know

  • Chain rule: d/dx[f(g(x))] = f′(g(x))·g′(x). Differentiate the outside function, keep the inside the same, then multiply by the derivative of the inside.
  • In Leibniz form: if y = f(u) and u = g(x), then dy/dx = (dy/du)(du/dx). Chains can be long: sin³(4x) needs three links.
  • Common chain-rule patterns: d/dx[e^(g(x))] = e^(g(x))·g′(x); d/dx[ln(g(x))] = g′(x)/g(x); d/dx[(g(x))ⁿ] = n(g(x))ⁿ⁻¹·g′(x).
  • Implicit differentiation: differentiate both sides with respect to x, treating y as a function of x. Every time you differentiate a y-term, multiply by dy/dx. Then solve for dy/dx.
  • For an implicit curve, horizontal tangents occur where the numerator of dy/dx is 0 (and the denominator is not), vertical tangents where the denominator is 0 (and the numerator is not).
  • Second derivatives of implicit curves: differentiate dy/dx again, then substitute the expression for dy/dx back in.
  • If g is the inverse of f, then g′(b) = 1 ÷ f′(a), where f(a) = b. Find a first, by solving f(a) = b; you rarely need a formula for g.
  • Inverse trig derivatives: d/dx[arcsin x] = 1/√(1 − x²), d/dx[arccos x] = −1/√(1 − x²), d/dx[arctan x] = 1/(1 + x²). Use the chain rule when the input is not just x.
  • Other bases: d/dx[aˣ] = aˣ·ln a and d/dx[logₐ x] = 1/(x·ln a).
  • Higher-order derivatives: f″ is the derivative of f′, f‴ of f″. In motion, position → velocity → acceleration.

Formulas

  • d/dx f(g(x)) = f′(g(x))·g′(x)
  • d/dx e^u = e^u·u′; d/dx ln u = u′/u
  • (f⁻¹)′(b) = 1 / f′(a), where f(a) = b
  • d/dx arcsin x = 1/√(1−x²); d/dx arctan x = 1/(1+x²)
  • d/dx aˣ = aˣ ln a

Exam traps

Watch forForgetting the inside derivative is the single most common calculus mistake. d/dx[sin(3x)] is 3cos(3x), not cos(3x).
Watch forWhen differentiating implicitly, y² becomes 2y·dy/dx, not 2y. And xy needs the product rule: y + x·dy/dx.
Watch forFor an inverse function, evaluate f′ at a (the x-value where f(a) = b), not at b.
Watch ford/dx[ln(5x)] = 1/x, not 5/x: the 5 cancels (5/(5x)).
Watch for(sin x)² and sin(x²) are different: their derivatives are 2sin x cos x and 2x cos(x²).

Key terms

  • Chain rule: d/dx f(g(x)) = f′(g(x))·g′(x): outside derivative times inside derivative.
  • Leibniz chain rule: dy/dx = (dy/du)(du/dx).
  • d/dx e^u: e^u·u′.
  • d/dx ln u: u′/u.
  • d/dx aˣ: aˣ·ln a.
  • d/dx logₐ x: 1/(x·ln a).
  • Implicit differentiation: Differentiate both sides with respect to x, multiply each y-term's derivative by dy/dx, then solve for dy/dx.
  • Horizontal tangent (implicit): Where the numerator of dy/dx is 0 and the denominator is not.
  • Vertical tangent (implicit): Where the denominator of dy/dx is 0 and the numerator is not.
  • Derivative of an inverse function: If g = f⁻¹ and f(a) = b, then g′(b) = 1/f′(a).
  • d/dx arcsin x: 1/√(1 − x²).
  • d/dx arccos x: −1/√(1 − x²).
  • d/dx arctan x: 1/(1 + x²).
  • Second derivative: f″, the derivative of f′. It measures how the slope changes.

Practice questions

Five of the 70 Unit 3 questions in Credit Sprint. Every choice is explained in the app.

1. What is d/dx [sin(x²)]?
  1. cos(x²)
  2. 2 sin x cos x
  3. 2x cos(x²)
  4. 2x cos(2x)
Show answer

2x cos(x²). Chain rule: the outside derivative is cos(x²), times the inside derivative 2x.

2. f and g are inverses, f(2) = 5 and f′(2) = 4. What is g′(5)?
  1. 1/4
  2. 4
  3. 1/5
  4. −1/4
Show answer

1/4. g′(5) = 1 ÷ f′(g(5)) = 1 ÷ f′(2) = 1/4.

3. g(1) = 5, g′(1) = 2, f′(5) = −3 and f′(1) = −1. If h(x) = f(g(x)), what is h′(1)?
  1. −6
  2. −2
  3. −3
  4. −1
Show answer

−6. Chain rule: h′(1) = f′(g(1))·g′(1) = f′(5)·2 = (−3)(2) = −6.

4. g(2) = 4, g′(2) = 4, f′(4) = 1 and f′(2) = −2. If h(x) = f(g(x)), what is h′(2)?
  1. 1
  2. −8
  3. 4
  4. 5
Show answer

4. Chain rule: h′(2) = f′(g(2))·g′(2) = f′(4)·4 = (1)(4) = 4.

5. If f(x) = (4x − 3)², what is f′(0)?
  1. −6
  2. 8
  3. −24
  4. 9
Show answer

−24. f′(x) = 2(4x − 3)·4. At x = 0 the inside is −3, so f′ = 2·-3·4 = −24.
