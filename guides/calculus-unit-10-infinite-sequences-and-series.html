Credit Sprint's Guide to AP® Calculus BC, Unit 10: Infinite Sequences and Series

BC weight 15–20%

Unit 10 is the biggest BC-only unit. You decide whether an infinite series converges, using a toolbox of tests; find sums of geometric series; bound the error of an approximation; and build Taylor and Maclaurin polynomials and series, finding their intervals and radii of convergence.

What you need to know

  • A series Σaₙ converges if its sequence of partial sums Sₙ approaches a finite limit. Otherwise it diverges.
  • nth-term test: if lim aₙ ≠ 0, the series diverges. If lim aₙ = 0, the test tells you nothing.
  • Geometric series Σ a·rⁿ converges to a/(1 − r), where a is the first term, when |r| < 1, and diverges when |r| ≥ 1.
  • p-series Σ1/nᵖ converges when p > 1 and diverges when p ≤ 1. The harmonic series Σ1/n diverges.
  • Integral test: if f is positive, continuous and decreasing with f(n) = aₙ, then Σaₙ and ∫₁^∞ f(x) dx both converge or both diverge.
  • Comparison tests: compare to a known series. Direct comparison needs the inequality to point the right way; limit comparison needs lim aₙ/bₙ to be a positive finite number.
  • Alternating series test: Σ(−1)ⁿbₙ converges if bₙ decreases and lim bₙ = 0. The error after n terms is at most the first omitted term, bₙ₊₁.
  • Ratio test: if lim |aₙ₊₁/aₙ| = L, the series converges absolutely when L < 1 and diverges when L > 1; L = 1 is inconclusive. It is the go-to test for power series.
  • Absolute convergence (Σ|aₙ| converges) implies convergence. Conditional convergence means Σaₙ converges but Σ|aₙ| diverges, as for the alternating harmonic series.
  • A Taylor polynomial of degree n centered at x = a is Pₙ(x) = Σ f⁽ᵏ⁾(a)(x − a)ᵏ/k!, k = 0 to n. A Maclaurin polynomial is centered at 0.
  • The Lagrange error bound: |f(x) − Pₙ(x)| ≤ max|f⁽ⁿ⁺¹⁾(z)|·|x − a|ⁿ⁺¹/(n + 1)!, with the maximum taken between a and x.
  • Know these Maclaurin series: eˣ = Σxⁿ/n!; sin x = x − x³/3! + x⁵/5! − …; cos x = 1 − x²/2! + x⁴/4! − …; 1/(1 − x) = Σxⁿ for |x| < 1.
  • Build new series by substituting (e^(−x²): replace x with −x²), multiplying by powers of x, and differentiating or integrating term by term. The radius of convergence stays the same; the endpoints can change.
  • Radius and interval of convergence: use the ratio test to find |x − a| < R, then test each endpoint separately with another test.

Formulas

  • Geometric: Σ a·rⁿ = a/(1 − r), |r| < 1
  • p-series converges ⇔ p > 1
  • Ratio test: L = lim |aₙ₊₁/aₙ| < 1 ⇒ converges
  • Alternating error ≤ |first omitted term|
  • Pₙ(x) = Σ f⁽ᵏ⁾(a)(x − a)ᵏ / k!
  • Lagrange: |Rₙ(x)| ≤ M|x − a|ⁿ⁺¹/(n + 1)!

Exam traps

Watch forlim aₙ = 0 does not mean the series converges. The harmonic series is the counterexample.
Watch forAlways check the endpoints of an interval of convergence separately; the ratio test is inconclusive there.
Watch forA Taylor coefficient is f⁽ⁿ⁾(a)/n!, not f⁽ⁿ⁾(a). Forgetting the factorial is the classic error.
Watch forFor a geometric series the first term matters: Σ from n = 1 of (1/2)ⁿ is 1, not 2.
Watch forWhen you use the alternating series error bound, show that the terms decrease to 0 first.

Key terms

  • Infinite series: A sum Σaₙ of infinitely many terms; it converges if its partial sums approach a finite limit.
  • Partial sum: Sₙ = a₁ + a₂ + … + aₙ.
  • nth-term test: If lim aₙ ≠ 0, the series diverges. If the limit is 0, the test is inconclusive.
  • Geometric series: Σ a·rⁿ converges to a/(1 − r) when |r| < 1; diverges when |r| ≥ 1.
  • Harmonic series: Σ1/n, which diverges.
  • p-series: Σ1/nᵖ converges for p > 1, diverges for p ≤ 1.
  • Integral test: For positive, decreasing, continuous f with f(n) = aₙ: Σaₙ and ∫₁^∞ f(x) dx both converge or both diverge.
  • Direct comparison test: If 0 ≤ aₙ ≤ bₙ and Σbₙ converges, so does Σaₙ; if Σaₙ diverges, so does Σbₙ.
  • Limit comparison test: If lim aₙ/bₙ is positive and finite, the two series both converge or both diverge.
  • Alternating series test: Σ(−1)ⁿbₙ converges if bₙ decreases and lim bₙ = 0.
  • Alternating series error bound: The error after n terms is at most the first omitted term's absolute value.
  • Ratio test: L = lim |aₙ₊₁/aₙ|: L < 1 converges absolutely, L > 1 diverges, L = 1 inconclusive.
  • Absolute convergence: Σ|aₙ| converges; this implies Σaₙ converges.
  • Conditional convergence: Σaₙ converges but Σ|aₙ| diverges, like the alternating harmonic series.
  • Taylor polynomial: Pₙ(x) = Σ f⁽ᵏ⁾(a)(x − a)ᵏ/k!, k = 0 to n.
  • Maclaurin series: A Taylor series centered at 0.

Practice questions

Five of the 75 Unit 10 questions in Credit Sprint. Every choice is explained in the app.

1. Which statement is true about Σ from n = 1 to ∞ of 1/n?
  1. It diverges, even though 1/n → 0
  2. It converges by the alternating series test
  3. It converges to 1
  4. It converges because the terms go to 0
Show answer

It diverges, even though 1/n → 0. The harmonic series is the classic counterexample: terms going to 0 is not enough for convergence.

2. The series for sin x is used with terms through x⁵ to estimate sin(0.5). What bounds the error by the alternating series error bound?
  1. 0
  2. (0.5)⁷/7!
  3. (0.5)⁶/6!
  4. (0.5)⁵/5!
Show answer

(0.5)⁷/7!. The first omitted term is −x⁷/7!, so the error is at most (0.5)⁷/7!.

3. What is the sum of the series 1 + 1/3 + 1/9 + 1/27 + …?
  1. 1/2
  2. 3/2
  3. 3/4
  4. It diverges
Show answer

3/2. A geometric series with first term 1 and ratio 1/3; |r| < 1, so the sum is 1 ÷ (1 − (1/3)) = 3/2.

4. What is the sum of the series 2 − 1 + 1/2 − 1/4 + …?
  1. 4
  2. −2/3
  3. 4/3
  4. It diverges
Show answer

4/3. A geometric series with first term 2 and ratio −1/2; |r| < 1, so the sum is 2 ÷ (1 − (−1/2)) = 4/3.

5. What is the sum of the series 3 + 2 + 4/3 + 8/9 + …?
  1. 9/5
  2. 6
  3. It diverges
  4. 9
Show answer

9. A geometric series with first term 3 and ratio 2/3; |r| < 1, so the sum is 3 ÷ (1 − (2/3)) = 9.
