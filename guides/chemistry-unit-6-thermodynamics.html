Credit Sprint's Guide to Chemistry Unit 6: Thermodynamics

AP® weight 7–9%CLEP® weight 4–6%

Thermodynamics tracks energy changes. Heat is energy moved by a temperature difference, enthalpy is the heat at constant pressure, and Hess's law, formation enthalpies and bond energies let you calculate ΔH without measuring it.

What you need to know

  • Temperature measures average kinetic energy; heat q is energy transferred because of a temperature difference.
  • q = mcΔT. Heat lost by the hot object equals heat gained by the cold one at equilibrium.
  • Exothermic: ΔH < 0, heat leaves the system, surroundings warm up. Endothermic: ΔH > 0.
  • During a phase change temperature is constant; energy changes the potential energy of the particles (q = nΔHfus or nΔHvap).
  • Hess's law: ΔH is a state function. Reverse an equation and the sign flips; multiply an equation and ΔH multiplies.
  • ΔH°rxn = ΣΔH°f(products) − ΣΔH°f(reactants). ΔH°f of an element in its standard state is zero.
  • Bond energies: ΔH ≈ Σ(bonds broken) − Σ(bonds formed). Breaking bonds is endothermic.
  • Calorimetry measures q for a reaction from the temperature change of a known mass of water.
  • First law: ΔU = q + w; energy is conserved.

Equations and rules

q = mcΔT
q = nΔH(phase change)
ΔH°rxn = ΣΔH°f(products) − ΣΔH°f(reactants)
ΔH ≈ Σ(bonds broken) − Σ(bonds formed)

Exam traps

Watch forReversing a reaction changes the sign of ΔH.
Watch forThe coffee-cup calorimeter measures the surroundings, so q_rxn = −q_solution.
Watch forBond energy calculations are only estimates.
Watch forTemperature doesn't change during melting or boiling.

Key terms

  • Enthalpy change (ΔH): Heat transferred at constant pressure; negative for exothermic reactions.
  • Specific heat capacity: Heat needed to raise 1 g of a substance by 1 °C; q = mcΔT.
  • Hess's law: The enthalpy change of a reaction equals the sum of the enthalpy changes of its steps.
  • Standard enthalpy of formation: Enthalpy change to form 1 mol of a compound from its elements in standard states.
  • Bond energy: Energy needed to break one mole of a bond; breaking is endothermic.
  • Calorimetry: Measuring heat transfer from the temperature change of a known mass.
  • State function: A property that depends only on the current state, like enthalpy or entropy.
  • First law of thermodynamics: Energy is conserved: ΔU = q + w.

Practice questions

Five of the 48 Unit 6 questions in Credit Sprint. Every choice is explained in the app.

1. How much heat is needed to raise the temperature of 50.0 g of water from 25.0 °C to 35.0 °C? (c = 4.184 J/g·°C)
  1. 2090 J
  2. 5230 J
  3. 20,900 J
  4. 209 J
Show answer

2090 J. q = mcΔT = (50.0)(4.184)(10.0) = 2092 J.

2. Which expression correctly states Hess's law?
  1. ΔH of a reverse reaction has the same sign
  2. ΔH depends on the path taken
  3. The enthalpy change of a reaction is the sum of the enthalpy changes of its steps
  4. ΔH is always negative
Show answer

The enthalpy change of a reaction is the sum of the enthalpy changes of its steps. ΔH is a state function.

3. Breaking a chemical bond is
  1. endothermic
  2. neither
  3. spontaneous
  4. exothermic
Show answer

endothermic. Energy must be supplied.

4. A system absorbs 500 J of heat and does 200 J of work on the surroundings. What is the change in internal energy?
  1. +300 J
  2. +700 J
  3. −300 J
  4. +200 J
Show answer

+300 J. ΔU = q + w = 500 − 200 = +300 J.

5. If a reaction has ΔH = −100 kJ, what is ΔH for the reverse reaction?
  1. +100 kJ
  2. −100 kJ
  3. −50 kJ
  4. 0 kJ
Show answer

+100 kJ. Reversing a reaction changes the sign.
