Credit Sprint's Guide to AP® Human Geography, Unit 3: Cultural Patterns and Processes

AP® weight 12–17%

Culture is the shared beliefs, practices and material things of a group. Unit 3 covers cultural landscapes, how culture spreads by diffusion, the historic causes of today's cultural patterns, and the distribution and diffusion of languages and religions.

What you need to know

  • Culture includes cultural traits (a single element, such as a food or a holiday) and cultural complexes (a related set of traits). Folk culture is traditional, local and slow to change; popular culture is widespread, changes quickly and spreads through media and trade.
  • The cultural landscape is the visible imprint of human activity on the land: buildings, fields, signs, religious structures. Sequent occupance describes layers left by successive groups. Traditional and postmodern architecture, land use patterns and sacred sites all reveal culture.
  • Attitudes toward cultural difference: ethnocentrism judges other cultures by one's own standards; cultural relativism tries to understand a culture on its own terms. Centripetal forces unite a group, and centrifugal forces divide it.
  • Diffusion is the spread of an idea or trait. Relocation diffusion moves with people who migrate. Expansion diffusion spreads outward while staying at the source, in three types: contagious (to nearby people, like a disease), hierarchical (through ranks, such as from big cities or powerful people down) and stimulus (the idea spreads but changes, such as menu items adapted to local tastes). Reverse hierarchical diffusion moves from small places or ordinary people upward.
  • Historic forces shaped cultural patterns: colonialism and imperialism spread European languages and Christianity; trade spread Islam, Buddhism and goods; forced migration, including the Atlantic slave trade, carried African cultures to the Americas.
  • Globalization speeds diffusion. Acculturation is when a group adopts some traits of another while keeping its own; assimilation is the loss of the original culture; multiculturalism lets several cultures coexist; syncretism blends traits into something new (such as Vodou or Tex-Mex food).
  • Languages are grouped into families, branches and individual languages. Indo-European is the largest family by speakers. The Anatolian (farming spread from Turkey) and Kurgan (horse-riding nomads from the steppe) hypotheses explain its spread. A lingua franca is a common language for trade (English, Swahili). Creole languages form from mixed languages and become native tongues; pidgins are simplified contact languages. Dialects are regional varieties of one language. Isolated languages, such as Basque, have no known relatives.
  • Universalizing religions seek converts worldwide: Christianity, Islam, Buddhism and Sikhism. Ethnic religions are tied to a people and place and rarely seek converts: Hinduism, Judaism and traditional faiths such as Shinto. Christianity spread by relocation and hierarchical diffusion (through Roman leaders); Islam spread by trade and conquest from the Arabian Peninsula; Buddhism spread from India through Asia by missionaries and trade.
  • Sacred spaces (Jerusalem, Mecca, Varanasi, Bodh Gaya) and pilgrimage show religion in the landscape, and religious differences can drive conflict or unity.

Exam traps

Watch forRelocation diffusion needs people to MOVE and carry the trait. Expansion diffusion (contagious, hierarchical, stimulus) spreads while staying at the source.
Watch forStimulus diffusion is when the underlying idea spreads but the trait is changed to fit local culture. A fast-food chain selling vegetarian burgers in India is the classic example.
Watch forHinduism and Judaism are ethnic religions. Buddhism, Christianity, Islam and Sikhism are universalizing. Buddhism began from Hinduism but is universalizing.
Watch forAcculturation keeps the home culture while adding new traits. Assimilation means the original culture fades away.

Key terms

  • Culture: The shared beliefs, practices, language and material objects of a group.
  • Cultural trait: A single element of culture, such as a food, a tool or a custom.
  • Folk culture: Traditional culture of a small, often rural group that changes slowly.
  • Popular culture: Culture shared by large, diverse groups that changes quickly and spreads through media.
  • Cultural landscape: The visible imprint of human activity on the land.
  • Sequent occupance: The layers of cultural landscape left by successive groups living in one place.
  • Ethnocentrism: Judging other cultures by the standards of one's own.
  • Cultural relativism: Understanding a culture on its own terms.
  • Centripetal force: A force that unites a group or state, such as a shared language.
  • Centrifugal force: A force that divides a group or state, such as religious conflict.
  • Cultural hearth: A place where a culture or trait first developed.
  • Relocation diffusion: The spread of a trait when people move and carry it with them.
  • Expansion diffusion: The spread of a trait outward while it stays strong at its source.
  • Contagious diffusion: Expansion diffusion from person to person, like a disease.
  • Hierarchical diffusion: Expansion diffusion from more important people or places to less important ones.
  • Reverse hierarchical diffusion: Diffusion that starts with ordinary people or small places and moves up.

Practice questions

Five of the 62 Unit 3 questions in Credit Sprint. Every choice is explained in the app.

1. Which is the best example of folk culture?
  1. A global smartphone launch
  2. Handmade quilts sewn with patterns passed down in an Amish community
  3. A worldwide pop music hit
  4. A fast-food chain menu
Show answer

Handmade quilts sewn with patterns passed down in an Amish community. Folk culture is traditional, local and slow to change.

2. A tourist complains that a foreign city's food is 'weird' compared to food at home. This attitude is
  1. Cultural relativism
  2. Acculturation
  3. Ethnocentrism
  4. Syncretism
Show answer

Ethnocentrism. Judging another culture by one's own standards is ethnocentric.

3. A viral dance video spreads from person to person across social media regardless of status. This is
  1. Stimulus diffusion
  2. Relocation diffusion
  3. Hierarchical diffusion
  4. Contagious diffusion
Show answer

Contagious diffusion. It spreads rapidly to everyone nearby in a network, like a disease.

4. An immigrant family in the U.S. adopts American holidays and fast food but keeps its language at home. This is
  1. Acculturation
  2. Assimilation
  3. Ethnocentrism
  4. Syncretism
Show answer

Acculturation. They adopted some traits while keeping their own culture.

5. Swahili is widely used in East Africa for trade among speakers of different languages. Swahili serves as a
  1. Language isolate
  2. Lingua franca
  3. Dialect of English
  4. Creole
Show answer

Lingua franca. A common trade language among different language groups.
