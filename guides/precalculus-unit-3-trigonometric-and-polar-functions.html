Credit Sprint's Guide to AP® Precalculus, Unit 3: Trigonometric and Polar Functions

AP® weight 30–35%CLEP® about 30%

Unit 3 is about functions that repeat. You define sine, cosine and tangent on the unit circle, graph them and their transformations, build sinusoidal models of periodic data, use inverse trig functions and identities to solve equations, and graph curves in the polar coordinate system.

What you need to know

  • A periodic function repeats: f(x + p) = f(x) for every x, where p is the period.
  • Angles in standard position start on the positive x-axis. Positive angles turn counterclockwise. Radian measure is arc length on a unit circle: 180° = π radians.
  • On the unit circle, the point at angle θ is (cos θ, sin θ), and tan θ = sin θ ÷ cos θ is the slope of the terminal ray. For a point (x, y) at distance r: sin θ = y/r, cos θ = x/r, tan θ = y/x.
  • Memorize the special angles: π/6, π/4 and π/3 give sines 1/2, √2/2 and √3/2. Use reference angles and quadrant signs (All Students Take Calculus: all positive in I, sine in II, tangent in III, cosine in IV) for the rest.
  • The graphs of sine and cosine are sinusoids with amplitude 1, period 2π and midline y = 0. Cosine is sine shifted π/2 left.
  • For f(θ) = a sin(b(θ + c)) + d: amplitude |a|, period 2π/|b|, phase shift −c (left c), midline y = d. The maximum is d + |a| and the minimum is d − |a|.
  • To model periodic data, find the midline (average of max and min), amplitude (half the difference), period (max to next max), and a starting point for the phase shift.
  • Tangent has period π and vertical asymptotes where cos θ = 0 (θ = π/2 + kπ). Its graph increases between asymptotes.
  • Inverse trig functions undo sine, cosine and tangent on restricted domains: arcsin and arctan return angles in [−π/2, π/2] (arctan excludes the endpoints); arccos returns angles in [0, π].
  • Solving a trig equation usually gives infinitely many solutions; on an interval like [0, 2π), find every angle with the right value in each period.
  • Reciprocal functions: csc θ = 1/sin θ, sec θ = 1/cos θ, cot θ = 1/tan θ. Key identities: sin²θ + cos²θ = 1, 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ, sum identities, and double angles sin 2θ = 2 sin θ cos θ and cos 2θ = cos²θ − sin²θ.
  • Polar coordinates (r, θ) name a point by its distance and angle. Convert with x = r cos θ, y = r sin θ, r² = x² + y², tan θ = y/x.
  • Polar graphs: r = a is a circle; θ = c is a line through the pole; r = a cos(nθ) or a sin(nθ) is a rose with n petals (n odd) or 2n petals (n even); r = a + b cos θ is a limaçon. When r is positive and increasing, the curve moves away from the pole.

Formulas

  • 180° = π radians
  • sin θ = y/r, cos θ = x/r, tan θ = y/x
  • y = a sin(b(x + c)) + d: period 2π/|b|
  • sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 and 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ
  • sin(α ± β) = sin α cos β ± cos α sin β; cos(α ± β) = cos α cos β ∓ sin α sin β
  • x = r cos θ, y = r sin θ, r² = x² + y²

Exam traps

Watch forThe period of sin(bx) is 2π/b, not b or 2πb. For tangent it is π/b.
Watch forarcsin and arccos have restricted ranges. arccos(−1/2) is 2π/3, not 4π/3.
Watch forPhase shift in y = sin(2x − π) is π/2 right, not π: factor out the 2 first.
Watch forSet your calculator to radians unless a question uses degrees.
Watch forsin(2θ) is not 2 sin θ; use the double-angle identity.

Key terms

  • Periodic function: f(x + p) = f(x) for all x; p is the period.
  • Radian: The angle that cuts off an arc equal to the radius; 180° = π radians.
  • Standard position: Vertex at the origin, starting on the positive x-axis.
  • Coterminal angles: Angles with the same terminal side; they differ by multiples of 2π.
  • Reference angle: The acute angle between the terminal side and the x-axis.
  • Unit circle point: The point at angle θ is (cos θ, sin θ).
  • sin, cos, tan for a point (x, y): sin θ = y/r, cos θ = x/r, tan θ = y/x, where r = √(x² + y²).
  • sin(π/6), sin(π/4), sin(π/3): 1/2, √2/2, √3/2.
  • cos(π/6), cos(π/4), cos(π/3): √3/2, √2/2, 1/2.
  • tan(π/6), tan(π/4), tan(π/3): √3/3, 1, √3.
  • Quadrant signs: I: all positive. II: sine. III: tangent. IV: cosine.
  • Sinusoid: A transformation of sine or cosine: y = a sin(b(x + c)) + d.
  • Amplitude: |a|: half the distance from max to min.
  • Period of sin(bx) and cos(bx): 2π/|b|.
  • Period of tan(bx): π/|b|.
  • Midline: y = d: the average of the max and the min.

Practice questions

Five of the 129 Unit 3 questions in Credit Sprint. Every choice is explained in the app.

1. A function f is periodic with period 6. If f(2) = 5, what is f(20)?
  1. Not enough information
  2. 5
  3. 15
  4. 20
Show answer

5. 20 = 2 + 3·6, so f(20) = f(2) = 5.

2. Which identity is always true?
  1. sin²θ + cos²θ = 1
  2. sin θ + cos θ = 1
  3. sin(2θ) = 2 sin θ
  4. tan²θ + 1 = cos²θ
Show answer

sin²θ + cos²θ = 1. It is the Pythagorean theorem on the unit circle: x² + y² = 1.

3. Daily high temperatures in a city over a year are best modeled by…
  1. an exponential function
  2. a sinusoidal function with a period of about 365 days
  3. a linear function
  4. a sinusoid with a period of 24 hours
Show answer

a sinusoidal function with a period of about 365 days. Temperatures rise and fall in a repeating yearly cycle.

4. What is the exact value of cos(2π/3)?
  1. √3/2
  2. √2/2
  3. 1/2
  4. −1/2
Show answer

−1/2. On the unit circle, 2π/3 lands at (−1/2, √3/2). cos is the x-coordinate, so the value is −1/2.

5. What is the exact value of tan(11π/6)?
  1. −√3/3
  2. √3/3
  3. −1
  4. √3
Show answer

−√3/3. On the unit circle, 11π/6 lands at (√3/2, −1/2). tan = y ÷ x, so the value is −√3/3.
