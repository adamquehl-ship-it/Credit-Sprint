Credit Sprint's Guide to AP® Precalculus, Unit 1: Polynomial and Rational Functions

AP® weight 30–40%CLEP® about 35%

Precalculus starts with how functions change. You describe where a function increases, decreases and bends, compute average rates of change, and then study polynomials and rational functions in depth: zeros and multiplicity, end behavior, asymptotes and holes, transformations, and how to build and judge models from data.

What you need to know

  • A function is increasing on an interval when larger inputs give larger outputs, and decreasing when they give smaller outputs. Relative maximums and minimums happen where it switches between the two.
  • The average rate of change on [a, b] is [f(b) − f(a)] ÷ (b − a): the slope of the secant line. A linear function has a constant rate of change; a quadratic's rate of change changes at a constant rate.
  • Concavity describes how the rate of change behaves. Concave up: rates of change increase (the graph bends up). Concave down: rates of change decrease. A point of inflection is where concavity switches.
  • For equally spaced inputs, constant first differences mean linear, constant second differences mean quadratic, and constant nth differences mean a polynomial of degree n.
  • A polynomial of degree n has at most n real zeros and at most n − 1 relative extrema. Counting complex zeros with multiplicity, it has exactly n. Non-real zeros of a real polynomial come in conjugate pairs (a ± bi).
  • Multiplicity controls the graph at a zero: odd multiplicity crosses the x-axis, even multiplicity touches and turns. Higher multiplicity flattens the graph near the zero.
  • End behavior depends on the leading term axⁿ: even degree means both ends point the same way (up if a > 0); odd degree means opposite ends (rising to the right if a > 0).
  • Even functions satisfy f(−x) = f(x) and are symmetric about the y-axis. Odd functions satisfy f(−x) = −f(x) and are symmetric about the origin.
  • A rational function r = p/q has zeros where p = 0, vertical asymptotes where q = 0 after cancelling, and holes where a factor cancels. Compare degrees for end behavior: bigger denominator → y = 0; equal → ratio of leading coefficients; numerator one degree bigger → slant asymptote found by division.
  • Polynomial long division and synthetic division rewrite p(x) = d(x)·q(x) + r(x). The remainder theorem says dividing by (x − c) leaves remainder p(c); the factor theorem says (x − c) is a factor exactly when p(c) = 0.
  • The binomial theorem expands (a + b)ⁿ with coefficients from Pascal's triangle: the term with bᵏ is C(n, k)aⁿ⁻ᵏbᵏ.
  • Transformations of f: f(x) + k shifts up k; f(x − h) shifts right h; a·f(x) stretches vertically by |a| (and reflects over the x-axis if a < 0); f(bx) compresses horizontally by a factor of 1/|b| (and reflects over the y-axis if b < 0).
  • To model data, choose a function type that matches the pattern (rates of change, symmetry, turning points), use regression or key points to find parameters, check the residuals, and keep the domain realistic for the context.

Formulas

  • Average rate of change on [a, b]: [f(b) − f(a)] ÷ (b − a)
  • Remainder theorem: p(x) ÷ (x − c) leaves remainder p(c)
  • Binomial theorem: (a + b)ⁿ = Σ C(n, k)aⁿ⁻ᵏbᵏ
  • g(x) = a·f(b(x − h)) + k: stretch a, compress 1/b, shift h right, k up

Exam traps

Watch forA hole is not a vertical asymptote. Cancel common factors first; only factors left in the denominator give asymptotes.
Watch forf(x − 3) moves the graph right 3, not left. Changes inside the function act opposite to how they look.
Watch forRates of change that are positive but shrinking mean increasing and concave down, not decreasing.
Watch forA rational function can cross its horizontal asymptote in the middle of the graph. The asymptote only describes end behavior.
Watch forDon't drop complex zeros when counting degree: if 2 + i is a zero, so is 2 − i.

Key terms

  • Function: A rule that assigns each input exactly one output.
  • Increasing function: Larger inputs give larger outputs: a < b means f(a) < f(b).
  • Average rate of change: [f(b) − f(a)] ÷ (b − a): the slope of the secant line from a to b.
  • Concave up: The rate of change is increasing; the graph bends upward.
  • Concave down: The rate of change is decreasing; the graph bends downward.
  • Point of inflection: A point where the graph changes concavity.
  • Relative maximum: An output greater than all nearby outputs, where f switches from increasing to decreasing.
  • Absolute (global) maximum: The greatest output of the function on its whole domain or interval.
  • Degree of a polynomial: The highest power of x; it caps the number of real zeros (n) and turning points (n − 1).
  • Leading term: The term with the highest power; it decides end behavior.
  • End behavior: What f(x) does as x → ∞ and x → −∞.
  • Multiplicity: How many times a factor repeats. Odd: the graph crosses. Even: it touches and turns.
  • Complex conjugate zeros: Non-real zeros of a real polynomial come in pairs a + bi and a − bi.
  • Fundamental theorem of algebra: A degree-n polynomial has exactly n complex zeros, counting multiplicity.
  • Even function: f(−x) = f(x); symmetric about the y-axis.
  • Odd function: f(−x) = −f(x); symmetric about the origin.

Practice questions

Five of the 142 Unit 1 questions in Credit Sprint. Every choice is explained in the app.

1. The function f is increasing on an interval. Which must be true for any a < b in that interval?
  1. f(a) < f(b)
  2. f(a) > f(b)
  3. f(a) = f(b)
  4. f(b) − f(a) < 0
Show answer

f(a) < f(b). Increasing means larger inputs give larger outputs: as x goes up, f(x) goes up.

2. The graph of an odd function is symmetric with respect to…
  1. the line y = x
  2. the origin
  3. the y-axis
  4. the x-axis
Show answer

the origin. Odd means f(−x) = −f(x): rotating the graph 180° about the origin leaves it unchanged.

3. g(x) = −f(x). The graph of g is the graph of f…
  1. reflected over the x-axis
  2. reflected over the y-axis
  3. shifted down 1
  4. reflected over the line y = x
Show answer

reflected over the x-axis. Negating every output flips each point (x, y) to (x, −y).

4. A complex zero of a polynomial…
  1. must have even multiplicity
  2. always appears as an x-intercept
  3. means the graph has a vertical asymptote
  4. does not correspond to an x-intercept of the graph
Show answer

does not correspond to an x-intercept of the graph. x-intercepts are real zeros. Non-real zeros do not appear on the real coordinate plane.

5. For f(x) = −2x² + 6x − 2, what is the average rate of change of f on the interval [−2, 1]?
  1. −8
  2. 8
  3. 24
  4. −10
Show answer

8. Average rate of change = [f(1) − f(−2)] ÷ (1 − −2) = (2 − −22) ÷ 3 = 8.
