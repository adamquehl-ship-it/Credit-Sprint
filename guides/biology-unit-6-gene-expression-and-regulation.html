Credit Sprint's Guide to Biology Unit 6: Gene Expression and Regulation

AP® weight 12–16%CLEP® weight 10–14%

DNA is copied before division and read to make RNA and proteins. Cells turn genes on and off so different cells do different jobs, mutations change the code, and biotechnology lets scientists read and edit it.

What you need to know

  • DNA replication is semiconservative: each new double helix keeps one old strand. Helicase unwinds, primase adds a primer, DNA polymerase builds 5′→3′, ligase joins Okazaki fragments on the lagging strand.
  • Transcription (nucleus): RNA polymerase reads the template strand and builds mRNA 5′→3′.
  • Eukaryotic mRNA is processed: a 5′ cap, a poly-A tail and splicing out of introns. Alternative splicing lets one gene make several proteins.
  • Translation (ribosome): tRNA anticodons pair with mRNA codons, bringing amino acids. It starts at AUG and stops at a stop codon.
  • The genetic code is redundant (several codons for most amino acids) and nearly universal, evidence of common ancestry.
  • Prokaryotes regulate genes with operons. The lac operon is inducible (on when lactose is present); the trp operon is repressible (off when tryptophan is high).
  • Eukaryotes regulate with transcription factors, enhancers, DNA methylation and histone acetylation. All cells carry the same DNA but express different genes.
  • Point mutations: silent (same amino acid), missense (different amino acid), nonsense (early stop codon). Frameshifts (insertions or deletions not in threes) change every codon after them.
  • Biotechnology: gel electrophoresis separates DNA by size (small pieces travel farther), PCR copies DNA, and bacterial transformation inserts genes with plasmids.
  • Mutations are random; selection acts on the variation they produce.

Equations and rules

mRNA codon is complementary to the DNA template and matches the coding strand (U for T)
Number of possible codons = 4³ = 64

Exam traps

Watch forThe tRNA anticodon is complementary to the mRNA codon, so it matches the DNA template strand's sequence (with U for T).
Watch forIntrons are removed; exons are expressed.
Watch forIn gel electrophoresis, DNA (negatively charged) moves toward the positive electrode.

Key terms

  • Semiconservative replication: Each new DNA molecule has one old strand and one new strand.
  • DNA polymerase: Builds new DNA 5′→3′ and proofreads.
  • Okazaki fragments: Short pieces of the lagging strand, later joined by ligase.
  • Transcription: Making mRNA from a DNA template. RNA polymerase does the work.
  • Intron: A non-coding section removed from pre-mRNA.
  • Codon: Three mRNA bases that code for one amino acid or a stop.
  • Anticodon: Three tRNA bases that pair with an mRNA codon.
  • Operon: A cluster of bacterial genes under one promoter and operator.
  • Transcription factor: A protein that helps turn a gene on (or off) by binding DNA.
  • Frameshift mutation: An insertion or deletion that shifts the reading frame.
  • Nonsense mutation: A point mutation that creates an early stop codon.
  • PCR: Polymerase chain reaction: makes millions of copies of a DNA segment.
  • Gel electrophoresis: Separates DNA fragments by size. Small fragments move farthest.

Practice questions

Five of the 67 Unit 6 questions in Credit Sprint. Every choice is explained in the app.

1. Meselson and Stahl's experiment showed that DNA replication is
  1. random
  2. dispersive
  3. semiconservative
  4. conservative
Show answer

semiconservative. Each new molecule had one old and one new strand.

2. Which modification is made to eukaryotic pre-mRNA before it leaves the nucleus?
  1. Exons are removed
  2. Amino acids are attached
  3. Introns are removed and a 5′ cap and poly-A tail are added
  4. Thymine is added
Show answer

Introns are removed and a 5′ cap and poly-A tail are added. Processing protects mRNA and helps translation.

3. A point mutation changes a codon from UAU (tyrosine) to UAA (stop). This is a
  1. silent mutation
  2. frameshift mutation
  3. missense mutation
  4. nonsense mutation
Show answer

nonsense mutation. It creates an early stop codon.

4. Adding acetyl groups to histones usually
  1. tightens chromatin and silences genes
  2. changes the DNA sequence
  3. loosens chromatin and increases transcription
  4. destroys the histones
Show answer

loosens chromatin and increases transcription. Accessible DNA is easier to transcribe.

5. Restriction enzymes are useful in biotechnology because they
  1. cut DNA at specific recognition sequences
  2. join DNA fragments
  3. copy DNA
  4. translate mRNA
Show answer

cut DNA at specific recognition sequences. They make predictable fragments.
